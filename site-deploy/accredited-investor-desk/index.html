Accredit Your Investors In-House – SEBI's New Manager-Led Route

10 November 2026

Accrediting investors just got simpler. Portfolio Managers, AIF Managers and SIF AMCs can now accredit their investors themselves, without sending each one to an Accreditation Agency.

The change comes from the SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026, effective 15 October 2026, and SEBI Circular No. HO/19/34/11(6)2026-AFD-POD1/I/25244/2026 dated 5 November 2026. Here is what it means for you, and how to put it into practice from day one.

Who is an Accredited Investor?

An Accredited Investor is recognised as having the financial capacity to invest in certain products with a lower minimum investment or concessions from specific regulatory requirements. An investor qualifies on any one criterion below (Regulation 2(1)(ab), SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012).

InvestorQualifies with
Individuals, HUFs, family trusts, sole proprietorshipsAnnual income of at least ₹2 crore; or net worth of at least ₹7.5 crore (at least ₹3.75 crore in financial assets); or income of at least ₹1 crore plus net worth of at least ₹5 crore (at least ₹2.5 crore in financial assets); or securities market exposure of at least ₹5 crore
Body corporates, LLPs, other trustsNet worth of at least ₹50 crore, or securities market exposure of at least ₹20 crore
Partnership firms (and, alternatively, LLPs)Each partner independently meets the criteria applicable to it
Deemed Accredited InvestorsCentral and State Governments, specified government-backed agencies and funds, QIBs, multilateral agencies and Persons Resident Outside India

What has changed

  • Manager-led accreditation: AIF Managers, Portfolio Managers and SIF AMCs can determine and record an investor's accredited status at onboarding or during a scheme's tenure. The Accreditation Agency route continues.
  • Group recognition: a Manager's determination is recognised by other Managers in the same Group for AIFs, SIFs and PMS during its validity. Group Investment Advisers and Research Analysts may rely on it but cannot determine status themselves.
  • Three-year validity: accreditation lasts three years. An investor accredited at onboarding into a scheme stays accredited for the life of that scheme.
  • New criterion: securities market exposure now counts – demat holdings, mutual fund folios, unlisted securities in demat form, overseas securities investments and NPS.
  • Persons Resident Outside India are now deemed Accredited Investors; anti-money-laundering due diligence still applies.

What managers need in place

The new route comes with safeguards. A Manager that accredits investors must have:

  • A documented accreditation policy
  • Conflict-of-interest controls, separating accreditation from business development and distribution
  • An escalation and grievance redressal process
  • Internal audit and compliance review of the process
  • Data privacy and cybersecurity safeguards for investor information
  • A written record of each decision, the criterion relied on and the evidence, so it can be independently reviewed
Records must be kept for at least five years from the date of accreditation, or five years after the investor stops investing in the Manager's schemes, whichever is later.
