SEBI streamlines inspection of market intermediaries: what compliance teams should change in their evidence systems

SEBI's August 2026 inspection-streamlining initiative is a useful reminder that inspection readiness should be a continuous control, not a last-minute document exercise.

Published 07 Aug 2026Inspection readiness6 min read

The regulatory development

SEBI announced measures on August 7, 2026 to streamline inspection of market intermediaries. The development is relevant across the regulated-intermediary ecosystem because inspection evidence has to be organised and retrievable.

The six-part evidence chain

A practical model is: regulatory requirement → internal control → responsible owner → completion date → evidence → review/closure. This model works across recurring and event-driven compliance.

Do not build an 'inspection folder' at the end

Evidence should be generated as part of the ordinary compliance process. A submission acknowledgement, website screenshot, certificate, policy approval, client disclosure or audit report is more useful when its context is already recorded.

Exceptions should remain visible

Open gaps should have an owner, due date, remediation action and closure evidence. Removing the item from the dashboard just because the deadline has passed creates an incomplete audit trail.

ScaleSafe implementation

Use inspection-ready evidence tags on every compliance control. At any point the system should be able to answer: which requirement, what action, who completed it, when, and where is the proof?
